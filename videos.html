THROWBACK / NIKE 6.0

Writing on the Wall: The Film That Defined a Generation

Released in 2008, Nike 6.0's first full-length BMX film brought together four of the era's most exciting street riders.
If playback fails, watch the full film on Pinkbike ↗

Filmed and edited by Glenn "PP" Milligan, the project followed Garrett Reynolds, Dennis Enarson, Nigel Sylvester and Mike Spinner around the world in search of fresh spots, new lines and unforgettable clips.

Four riders. Four styles.

Garrett Reynolds brought heavy-hitting street riding, technical precision and fearless commitment. Dennis Enarson moved between street, transition and huge drops with creativity and confidence. Nigel Sylvester's unmistakable New York style made each line feel like part of a story. Mike Spinner's manuals, nose manuals, grinds and combinations showed just how compelling technical riding could be.

More than a BMX video

Cinematic filming, polished editing and a carefully chosen soundtrack helped Writing on the Wall stand apart from the BMX videos of its time. It captured the travel, personalities and lifestyle around the riding, not just the tricks.

Nearly two decades later, the riding still feels fresh. The film remains a snapshot of four riders who went on to leave their own mark on BMX, and of an era when a crew, a camera and a search for new spots could become something unforgettable.

THROWBACK / RIDER PROFILE

Mike Miller: Style That Still Inspires

Few riders have influenced BMX quite like Mike Miller. His effortless style, technical riding and creative approach made him one of the standout names of the 2000s.

In a classic Ride UK interview, Mike explained that he never set out to become one of BMX's biggest names. He simply loved riding his bike. That passion, alongside a supportive local scene and countless hours on his bike, helped shape the smooth, creative style that inspired a generation.

The BMX community around Tadley and Basingstoke played a big part too. Riders pushed each other to improve without turning every session into a competition. It was a scene built on encouragement, creativity and progression - values that still define great BMX communities today.

His video parts still hold up today. Sit back, enjoy the riding, and remember why style never goes out of fashion.
