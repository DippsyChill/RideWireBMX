TRICK PROGRESSION · 27 AUG 2026

BACKFLIP
TO FRONTFLIP.

Ryan “R-Willy” Williams turns a five-year idea into a BMX sequence no one had landed before.

← Back to all news

Ryan Williams has landed a backflip-to-frontflip sequence that had not been seen before. The Australian rider’s concept begins with a backflip, pauses upside down against a raised container, then changes rotation into a half frontflip back toward the landing.

A five-year idea, built at full scale

Williams said the concept had been in his mind for about five years. Making it possible meant turning the landing into an obstacle: a crane suspended a shipping container above his mega ramp. The container gave him a surface to contact while inverted and a point around which to redirect his rotation.

That is what makes the trick so unusual. It is not simply two flips linked in open air; it uses an elevated stall to interrupt the backflip and create the transition into the frontflip. Williams has said the trick does not yet have a settled name.

Progression beyond the contest run

The stunt sits outside a standard competition format, but it reflects the same BMX impulse to find a new line and make it repeatable. The set-up demanded engineering, timing and a precise change in body position, with a considerable consequence for getting the sequence wrong.

For fans, the clip is a reminder that BMX progression can happen in custom projects as well as in a judged final. Williams’ next challenge may be deciding what to call a trick that needed a crane and a shipping container to exist.
