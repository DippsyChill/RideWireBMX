WHAT RIDEWIRE BMX IS ABOUT

BMX STORIES.
SCENE FIRST.

RideWire BMX is a one-person BMX media project built around the riders, places and moments that make the scene matter.

The site brings together BMX news, rider spotlights, throwback films and scene history. Freestyle is our main focus, with BMX Racing covered too—from major contests and results to the local places and communities behind them.

We look beyond the scoreboards to tell the stories around the riding: the people who build a scene, the videos that stay with us and the parks that shape generations of riders.

RideWire is growing story by story. Where we use outside reporting or photography, we aim to credit the source and link back so readers can follow the trail.

THE PERSON BEHIND THE SITE

ONE CREATOR.
ONE EDITOR.

I’m Karl, the sole creator and editor of RideWire BMX. Long before I started building this site, riding had already become a big part of my life.

As a kid, I started taking to the ramps at Parque da Juventude in Portimão in 2005, around the time of BMX Summer Comp. That’s where I met the old CBP Crew—people who became friends for life. BMX had a prominent, energetic scene in Portugal, and I was lucky to be part of it.

I loved riding in Portimão and travelling to demos and competitions around the country. I never placed near the top, but that was never what made those trips matter to me. The riding, the people and the time spent together were enough.

Then a fall that seemed simple at first brought a health condition to light. It continued to affect me and limited how often I could ride. I couldn’t get on my bike as much as I had always wanted, but I never lost my love for the ride.

I used to love sites like BMXAtitude, which is unfortunately offline now. I loved having BMX news, videos, events and stories together in one place. There are many BMX sites today, but I had always wanted to take a journey of my own and create a place that could help people stay connected to the sport.

When I can’t hit the ramps like I used to, RideWire gives me another way to stay close to BMX: following the scene, sharing rider stories and keeping the videos and memories that matter in view.

Around Portimão, many people knew me as “Dip”—and some used the longer nickname “Dipshit.” Both take me straight back to those days with the old CBP Crew.
